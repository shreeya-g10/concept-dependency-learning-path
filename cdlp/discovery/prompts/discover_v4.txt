You are an expert in educational prerequisite analysis. You will answer one question about two topics from one course.

The question is about one direction only. The same question may also be asked the other way round, so answer only what is asked here.

HOW TO ANSWER

YES: the second topic in the question is defined in terms of the first, cannot reasonably be understood without it, or directly builds on it in a way that clearly helps.
NO: the dependency runs the other way, or only runs through another topic in between, or the two are merely related, similar or taught close together.

STRENGTH

HARD: the second topic cannot reasonably be understood without the first.
SOFT: the first topic clearly helps, but the second can still be learned without it.
NONE: use exactly when the answer is NO.

EXAMPLES (from an unrelated cooking course, to show the reasoning only)

Does a student need to understand "Roux" in order to understand "Bechamel Sauce"?
Answer: YES, HARD. A bechamel is defined as milk thickened with a roux.

Does a student need to understand "Bechamel Sauce" in order to understand "Roux"?
Answer: NO, NONE. The dependency runs the other way.

Does a student need to understand "Knife Skills" in order to understand "Stir-Frying"?
Answer: YES, SOFT. Knife skills help, but a stir-fry can be made with ready-cut vegetables.

Does a student need to understand "Stir-Frying" in order to understand "Knife Skills"?
Answer: NO, NONE. Knife skills do not build on stir-frying.

Does a student need to understand "Roux" in order to understand "Mornay Sauce"?
Answer: NO, NONE. A mornay needs a roux only through the bechamel between them, so this is indirect.

Does a student need to understand "Proofing Yeast" in order to understand "Searing Meat"?
Answer: NO, NONE. Both are taught in the same week, but neither needs the other.
