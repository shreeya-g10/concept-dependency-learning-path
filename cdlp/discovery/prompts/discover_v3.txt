You are an expert in educational prerequisite analysis. You will compare two concepts from one course, called A and B.

A and B are ordered alphabetically by ID. That order says nothing about which concept is learned first.

POSSIBLE ANSWERS

A_BEFORE_B: A is a direct prerequisite of B.
B_BEFORE_A: B is a direct prerequisite of A.
NEITHER: there is no direct prerequisite either way.

STRENGTH (for A_BEFORE_B or B_BEFORE_A)

HARD: the later concept is defined in terms of the earlier one, or cannot reasonably be understood without it.
SOFT: the earlier concept clearly helps, but the later one can still be learned without it. Choose SOFT whenever the later concept can be learned without the earlier one.
NONE: use exactly when the answer is NEITHER.

CONFIDENCE

HIGH: the evidence states the dependency outright.
MEDIUM: the evidence implies it.
LOW: the evidence is thin and you are mostly reasoning from the meaning of the concepts.

EXAMPLES (from an unrelated cooking course, to show the reasoning only)

A "Bechamel Sauce", B "Roux". A bechamel is defined as milk thickened with a roux.
Answer: B_BEFORE_A, HARD, HIGH.

A "Knife Skills", B "Stir-Frying". Knife skills help, but a stir-fry can be made with ready-cut vegetables.
Answer: A_BEFORE_B, SOFT, MEDIUM.

A "Pasta Dough", B "Ravioli". Ravioli are parcels made from pasta dough.
Answer: A_BEFORE_B, HARD, HIGH.

A "Risotto", B "Stock Making". Good stock helps, but risotto can be learned with shop-bought stock.
Answer: B_BEFORE_A, SOFT, LOW.

A "Mornay Sauce", B "Roux". A mornay needs a roux only through the bechamel between them.
Answer: NEITHER, NONE, MEDIUM. An indirect prerequisite is not a direct one.

A "Proofing Yeast", B "Searing Meat". Both are taught in the same week, but neither needs the other.
Answer: NEITHER, NONE, HIGH. Being taught together, being similar or being taught earlier is not a prerequisite.
